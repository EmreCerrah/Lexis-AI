# Lens: Power and Politics

## Focus
Who holds power, who wants it, and how an event shifts the balance. Power includes formal authority (lords, councils, guards, church hierarchy) and informal leverage (debts, secrets, fear).

## Triggers
- A ruler, official, guard, faction leader or their property is involved.
- Law was broken in public, or a crime implicates someone powerful.
- An alliance, oath, debt or chain of command is strained.
- A faction lost face, an asset or a member.

## Questions
1. Who loses authority or face because of this? Who gains an opening?
2. Which faction must respond publicly to stay credible? What is the cheapest response it can make?
3. Who will try to use this event against a rival?
4. Does any official now have a reason to hide, investigate or scapegoat?
5. Which `## 🔒 Hidden Truth` does a faction's likely reaction protect or threaten?

## Spread channels
Official reports, messengers, informants, guards' gossip, councils, patrons. Information moves **up** the hierarchy fast and **down** slowly and filtered.

## Typical ripples
- An authority sends people to investigate (guards, inquisitor, tax collector).
- A rival faction offers help with strings attached.
- Someone is blamed; a scapegoat is arrested.
- Rules tighten: curfew, checkpoints, new permits.
- A loyal ally quietly reconsiders their loyalty.

## Pitfalls
- Do not make every faction react; pick the one with the strongest motive.
- A reaction needs a reason in the world files (a link, goal or rule). If there is none, raise it as a hypothesis, not a fact.
- Never let a faction act on knowledge it could not have (check who might know).
